# What Damodaran material exists for each Topic

Research for ticket #6. Checked on 2026-11-27 against Aswath Damodaran's own site (`pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/`), his YouTube channel and his blog. "Class" below always means one of his originals.

## The answer

**He has a class for every Topic.** Three match closely, one is two of his classes side by side, and one is a loose fit.

| Topic | His class, under his name | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting 101 | **Accounting** (on YouTube: "Accounting 101 (taught by a non-accountant)") | Close. Online only. |
| Statistics for Finance | **Statistics** (on YouTube: "Statistics 101") | Close. Online only. |
| Fundamentals of Finance | **Foundations of Finance** | Close. Different word, same job: he calls it the first class to take with no finance background. Online only. |
| Corporate Finance and Valuation | **Corporate Finance** and **Valuation**, two separate classes | Close, but it is two full classes. Each exists as a short online class, a full MBA class, an undergraduate class and a two-to-three-day executive class. |
| Portfolio Management | **Investment Philosophies** | Loose. He has no class called Portfolio Management. His site files this class under the heading "Portfolio Management", but it teaches how to choose a way of investing (value, growth, trading on news, market timing, indexing), not how to build and run a portfolio. |

**The short online classes are the right base.** He made them for people learning alone: one idea per session, 10 to 35 minutes of video, one slide deck, one short test with worked answers. Across the six online classes that is 139 sessions, about 40 hours of video, 140 slide decks and 136 tests. The MBA classes cover the same ground in 26 lectures of 80 minutes each, with slides that only come as packets running to more than 300 pages. Use those for current numbers and examples, not as the skeleton.

**What exists for one session of an online class:**

- a video on YouTube;
- a slide deck as a PDF of about a dozen slides, with real text in it (not a scan);
- a post-class test and its solution, as short PDFs;
- no transcript. He publishes none. YouTube's automatic captions are on every video I sampled; captions written by a person were on the two Corporate Finance Online videos I sampled and on nothing else.

**Cost of Capital is a block of ten sessions, not one.** In Corporate Finance Online it is sessions 4 to 13 ("Hurdle Rates I" to "Hurdle Rates X"): 2 hours 37 minutes of video, ten decks, ten tests. He teaches it again, compressed to four sessions and about an hour, in Valuation Online. Around those sit eight older webcasts made only about cost of capital (3 hours, 2012), five worked-example webcasts on real companies, the MBA lectures (about 9 hours across seven Corporate Finance lectures, 7 hours across five Valuation lectures), a dozen spreadsheets and a dozen datasets. The order is always the same: what risk is, risk-free rate, equity risk premium, beta, cost of equity, cost of debt, weights, the final number. The full list is in [section 3](#3-cost-of-capital-in-depth).

**What is awkward:**

- Almost every spreadsheet and dataset is an old-format Excel file (`.xls`).
- The numbers change every January. His datasets were last refreshed on 9 January 2026 and the next refresh is early January 2027. The equity risk premium file changes every month. File names stay the same when the contents change.
- The material comes from different years: the online Corporate Finance and Valuation classes are from 2014, Accounting and Foundations from 2020, Statistics from 2021, Investment Philosophies from 2026, the latest MBA lectures from spring 2025. His method has moved since 2014 in places.
- He did not teach in spring 2026 (a sabbatical). The next MBA classes start on 25 January 2027 and will bring new slides.
- Some links on his pages are broken or point to the wrong file.

### What this means for how the Course is structured

1. **His sessions cut cleanly.** Each online session already has one subject, one deck and one test. Nothing needs to be cut out of the middle of a lecture.
2. **A Lesson is bigger than one of his sessions.** The one Lesson on the map, Cost of Capital, equals ten of his sessions. If every Lesson is that size, the Course holds roughly 40 to 47 Lessons. If a Lesson were one of his sessions, it would hold 139.

| Topic | His sessions | Video | Natural groups in his own order | Lessons at Cost of Capital size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting 101 | 11 | 3.1 h | Six subjects, five of them taught as a pair (idea, then examples) | 5 to 6 |
| Statistics for Finance | 13 | 5.2 h | Six subjects, five of them followed by one or two example sessions | 5 to 6 |
| Fundamentals of Finance | 12 | 2.9 h | Business and cash flows; risk; time value of money; valuing bonds, shares and options; inflation, interest rates and exchange rates | 5 to 6 |
| Corporate Finance and Valuation | 61 (36 + 25) | 16.3 h | Corporate Finance has seven blocks. Valuation adds about seven more once its repeat of cost of capital is removed. | 13 to 16 |
| Portfolio Management | 42 | about 12.5 h | Fourteen groups, following his book's chapters | 11 to 13 |

3. **Corporate Finance and Valuation is by far the largest Topic**, close to half of all sessions. It teaches cost of capital twice. The Course needs it once.
4. **Cost of Capital sits early in that Topic** (sessions 4 to 13 of 36) and leans on all three Topics before it. A Student needs present value, risk and bonds from Fundamentals of Finance, regression from Statistics for Finance, and debt, leases and operating income from Accounting 101. The map's Topic order supports that.
5. **Numbers must be kept apart from explanations.** The explanations are stable for years. The risk-free rate, the equity risk premium, industry betas and default spreads change every January or every month. A Lesson that bakes his 2026 numbers into its text is out of date within weeks of January.
6. **The Chatbot has slides and tests as clean text, but no clean lecture text.** What he says in the videos, which is most of the teaching, exists only as automatic captions.

### 1. Which class matches each Topic

His own list of online classes names seven: Accounting, Statistics, Foundations of Finance, Corporate Finance Online, Valuation Online, Investment Philosophies Online and The Corporate Life Cycle. Source: [Online Classes](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/onlineclass.htm).

That page lists the first three in the same order as the Topics. A [blog post of December 2021](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2021/12/back-in-classroom-time-to-teach.html) lists them as Accounting, Foundations of Finance, Statistics.

He says the Accounting, Statistics and Foundations classes exist only online and are free. Source: [blog post, 11 December 2024](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/12/for-fun-of-it-open-house-for-my-spring.html).

**Portfolio Management.** His [Topics page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/topics.html) splits finance into corporate finance, valuation and portfolio management, and under Portfolio Management the "MBA Class" link goes to Investment Philosophies. An older [Investment Management page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/invemgmt/invmg.htm) says "I do not teach a class on investment philosophies but have the material ready for one" and holds old static pages on asset allocation, security selection, trading and performance evaluation. Those pages are the only material on the mechanics of running a portfolio. There are no sessions, videos or tests for them.

Why the online versions are the best base for beginners:

- He built them for the purpose. His words: "an 80-minute lecture, which may be palatable in a regular class, is much more difficult to take online" ([Online Classes](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/onlineclass.htm)).
- He says Corporate Finance Online "covers what I do in a regular MBA class" and Valuation Online "mirrors the regular MBA class".
- Every session has its own deck and its own test. The MBA classes have slide packets and a test per lecture, but a lecture runs across several subjects.
- He says the undergraduate class is "identical to the MBA class" and advises the latest MBA version over it ([Regular Class List](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/regularclass.htm)).

The cost of choosing the online versions: they are twelve years old. Their examples and numbers are from 2012 to 2014.

**Not taught in spring 2026.** His last email to the spring 2025 class says he is "taking the next year off as a sabbatical" and plans "to be back, rested and ready, in spring 2027" ([class emails](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/cfemail.html), entry of 14 May 2025). The spring 2026 class pages do not exist. The Regular Class List gives 25 January 2027 as the start of the next MBA classes. The spring 2027 class pages exist but list no sessions yet.

**One more class, with no Topic.** The Corporate Life Cycle (2024): 20 sessions and an overview, 10.1 hours, on how companies age. It cuts across corporate finance, valuation and investing. Source: [class page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/webcastCLC.htm).

### 4. What is awkward to use as it stands

**Slides**

- The decks are not scans. All three I opened have a real text layer.
- Charts and tables inside the slides are often pictures. One 11-slide deck held 37 embedded images. Numbers inside those are not readable as text.
- MBA slides come only as packets of several hundred pages (5 to 31 MB as PDF, up to 194 MB as PowerPoint). The per-lecture slide links on the 2025 class pages go to NYU's file-sharing service, not to his site.
- The 2012 webcasts and the worked examples use the old PowerPoint format (`.ppt`).

**Video**

- He publishes no transcripts.
- YouTube's automatic captions exist on all eight videos I sampled. Person-written captions exist only on the two Corporate Finance Online videos sampled.
- The 2012 cost of capital webcasts and the worked-example webcasts are large video files on his own server (84 to 222 MB each) with no captions at all.
- MBA recordings on NYU's own system need an NYU login. The YouTube copies do not.

**Old file formats**

- 69 of 73 spreadsheets and 240 of 246 dataset files are `.xls`, the Excel format replaced in 2007. `wacccalc.xls` was created in 2000 and last saved in 2015.
- A few introductory "podcasts" are `.mov` files. Some pages still link to iTunes U.
- He warns twice that Google Chrome has trouble downloading files from his site.

**Material that changes every January**

- Datasets: "updated in the first two weeks of every year". Last on 9 January 2026, next in early January 2027 ([Data page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/data.html)).
- The file names do not change. `wacc.xls` is this year's numbers now and will be next year's in January. Older years move to the archive page.
- The equity risk premium is recalculated monthly, in a file whose name changes with the month. Country risk is also updated in April and July.
- The MBA classes restart each spring with reissued packets and new class pages. The next start is 25 January 2027.

**Material spread across years**

- Corporate Finance Online and Valuation Online: 2014. Their slides and examples (Disney, HP, Home Depot) use 2012 and 2013 numbers.
- Cost of capital webcasts: 2012. Worked examples: 2013 and 2014.
- Accounting and Foundations: 2020. Statistics: 2021. Investment Philosophies: 2026. Latest MBA lectures: spring 2025.
- His method moves. One example: his [home page](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm) now offers a dollar risk-free rate that is the US Treasury rate less a US default spread (0.22%), with the premium adjusted to match. His [post of February 2026](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/02/data-update-4-for-2026-global.html) says his earlier approach ran "until May 2025". The 2014 sessions were recorded long before that change.
- The spring 2025 MBA pages still link 2013 files for some worked examples.

**Broken or misleading links found**

- Investment Philosophies session 25: the slide file is missing (the address returns "not found"), under both the new and the old folder.
- Investment Philosophies session 5: the class page links session 4's video.
- The risk-free rate worked example: its slide link on the Corporate Finance Online page is missing.
- Valuation MBA 2025: session 6's "YouTube" link goes to the file-sharing service, and session 26 links the Corporate Finance class's last video.
- The Accounting, Statistics and Foundations pages all carry the browser title "Corporate Finance Online Class".
- The Online Classes page says Foundations of Finance has nine sessions. The class page lists 12.
- Session numbers carry letters (2A, 5B) and Valuation Online's file numbers slip by one after session 19.

## Open questions

1. **How big is a Lesson?** Cost of Capital is ten of his sessions and seven steps. Is it one Lesson with seven steps on its path, or a group of Lessons? The answer sets the Course at roughly 40 to 47 Lessons or at 139.
2. **Which version is the base when two disagree?** The online classes give the clean session structure but are from 2014. The MBA lectures are current but long. Where his method has changed, which one does a Lesson teach?
3. **Which year's numbers does a Lesson show, and what happens each January?** His files are overwritten in place. Someone has to decide whether Lessons follow his latest numbers or stay on a fixed year.
4. **Where does the lecture text come from?** There are no transcripts. Automatic captions exist for YouTube videos, and nothing exists for the videos on his own server. This decides how much of his spoken teaching the Chatbot can draw on.
5. **Is Investment Philosophies the Portfolio Management Topic?** It is the only candidate, and it teaches a different thing from what the name suggests. Keep the name, rename the Topic, or add material from elsewhere.
6. **Does Corporate Finance and Valuation stay one Topic?** It is two classes, 61 sessions and about half the Course.
7. **Who notices broken links?** Several were found in a light pass. Loading his material needs a check that every file is where his page says it is.
